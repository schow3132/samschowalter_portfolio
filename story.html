My story

The Long Way Here.

My college transcript starts three years after most applicants'. That isn't a gap. Those were three years of learning how to work, how to live on my own, and how to be someone others can count on. They're the reason I'm a serious student today.

  1. Age 18 · Arizona

    On my own in Arizona

    After graduating from Eden Prairie High School, where I played varsity hockey, I wanted a reset. I took a spot as a goalie with the Arizona Jr. Coyotes and moved to Arizona at eighteen. For eight months I lived on my own: I managed my money, kept a schedule nobody was checking, and took responsibility for myself. I also found friends who truly supported me, and that helped me get back the confidence and happiness I'd been missing. I was named team captain, and we finished with top-three placements in multiple tournaments and second place in the regional standings.

  2. The following summer · CentiMark

    5 a.m. to 10 p.m.

    I came home and started as a warehouse associate at CentiMark, a national commercial roofing company, while continuing to train. My days were split: work at 5 a.m., training mid-day, then back to work until 10 p.m. That summer built the discipline I still rely on, the willingness to push through a day I'd rather not be in. It also earned me the respect of coworkers twice my age.

  3. Age 19 · St. Cloud

    Someone others can count on

    That fall I earned a spot with the Granite City Lumberjacks in St. Cloud. We won the West Division and took third place in the Fraser Cup. I lived with a host family who were going through a hard stretch. I was there for hockey, but most evenings I helped with their kids: making dinner, driving them to school, and showing up at their games when their parents couldn't. It taught me what it truly means to be dependable, and that the people who need that most rarely ask for it.

  4. Later that season

    A setback, and a decision

    A concussion left me with severe vertigo for over a month. When I recovered, I made a clear-eyed choice to step away from hockey and return to CentiMark. It wasn't the plan I'd started with, but it was the one I could commit to fully.

  5. Age 20 · CentiMark

    Running the job

    Back at CentiMark I moved from the warehouse floor to sole project coordinator on active industrial sites, and later Assistant Operations Manager. I stayed until I started school in fall 2025. I scheduled vendors, cranes, and crews across job sites, and I learned how much of a company's results are decided by everyday operating choices. A few things I'm proud of:

    • Changed how we ordered material, buying closer to when jobs needed it, which brought down inventory and freed up cash.
    • Helped manage a large branch's finances, tracking cost and revenue against every job each week.
    • Renegotiated equipment rental pricing with vendors, which improved our margins.
    • Led the closeout of a large industrial re-roof, working through the client's final items so the held-back payment could be released.

    The same warehouse, before and after we changed how we ordered material.

  6. Now · University of Minnesota

    A choice I made on purpose

    I didn't arrive at college by default. I paid for it, I chose it, and I understand what it costs. That's why I've packed so much in since: an analyst internship at a private equity firm, a student-managed fund, and a finance program I helped start.
