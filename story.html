My story

How I Got Here.

I started college a little later than most. Before that I played junior hockey and worked my way up at a roofing company, and those years shaped what I wanted to study. I've built my education around becoming both an operator and an investor.

  1. Age 18 · Arizona

    On my own in Arizona

    After graduating from Eden Prairie High School, where I played varsity hockey, I took a spot as a goalie with the Arizona Jr. Coyotes and moved to Arizona at eighteen. I lived on my own for eight months, which meant managing my own money, keeping a schedule nobody was checking, and taking responsibility for myself. I made great friends along the way. I was named team captain, and we finished with top-three placements in multiple tournaments and second place in the regional standings.

  2. The following summer · CentiMark

    5 a.m. to 10 p.m.

    I came home and started as a warehouse associate at CentiMark, a national commercial roofing company, while continuing to train. My days were split between work at 5 a.m., training mid-day, and back to work until 10 p.m. That summer built the discipline I still rely on, and it earned me the respect of coworkers twice my age. It was also my first look at how a business runs day to day.

  3. Age 19 · St. Cloud

    Someone others can count on

    That fall I earned a spot with the Granite City Lumberjacks in St. Cloud, where we won the West Division and took third place in the Fraser Cup. I lived with a host family, and most evenings I helped with their kids: making dinner, driving them to school, and showing up at their games when their parents couldn't. It taught me what it means to be dependable.

  4. Later that season

    A change of plans

    A concussion left me with severe vertigo for over a month. When I recovered, I decided to step away from hockey and return to CentiMark. It wasn't the plan I started with, but it was one I could commit to fully.

  5. Age 20 · CentiMark

    Running the job

    Back at CentiMark I moved from the warehouse floor to sole project coordinator on active industrial sites, and later Assistant Operations Manager, staying until I started school in fall 2025. I scheduled vendors, cranes, and crews across job sites, and I learned how much of a company's results are decided by everyday operating choices. That's where operating became a real passion of mine. A few things I'm proud of:

    • Changed how we ordered material, buying closer to when jobs needed it, which brought down inventory and freed up cash.
    • Helped manage a large branch's finances, tracking cost and revenue against every job each week.
    • Renegotiated equipment rental pricing with vendors, which improved our margins.
    • Led the closeout of a large industrial re-roof, working through the client's final items so the held-back payment could be released.

    The same warehouse, before and after we changed how we ordered material.

  6. Now · University of Minnesota

    Building an education around investing

    At CentiMark I got to operate. At Minnesota I've been adding the investor's side, and I've paid for school myself. I'm studying Business Economics with a minor in finance, and I've interned at a private equity firm, covered industrials for a student-managed fund, and helped start a program that prepares younger students for finance recruiting. My goal is to bring the two sides together in private equity.
