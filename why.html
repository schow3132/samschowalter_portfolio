Why I invest

Where Operating Meets Investing.

I want to be both an operator and an investor. Private equity is where the two come together.

I've come to think of myself as both an operator and an investor. At CentiMark I saw how a company runs up close: how it buys, schedules, and negotiates, and how those choices show up as cash. In school I've been learning the investor's side, how businesses are valued and how owners create value.

That's what led me to private equity. It's where my two passions come together: an owner has to choose the right business and then help make it better. I want to spend the next several years studying how the best investors do both.

Long term, I want to be a reputable investor: someone whose judgment people trust because it's grounded in a real understanding of businesses and how they're run. Right now that means learning widely, across different investing philosophies, different ideas about how owners create value, and the people who've done it well.

Questions I'm chasing

  1. What makes a business worth owning for a decade, not just a quarter?
  2. Where do the major investing philosophies agree, and where do they split?
  3. How does an owner create value after the deal closes, through operations, capital allocation, and people?
  4. What does it take to be an investor people trust, and to keep that trust over a career?

A line I keep coming back to

“But investing isn’t about beating others at their game. It’s about controlling yourself at your own game.”

From The Intelligent Investor by Benjamin Graham, with commentary by Jason Zweig

In my own words

Controlling What I Can.

One book I find really interesting is The Intelligent Investor, and the line above is one I really value. As an investor, I think it's an important idea. But it's just as applicable to life, and to how I go about mine today.

Hockey is a small world, and a world of comparisons. My mindset has changed. I focus on what I can control, and on improving myself to become a better individual and a better professional.
